How we tested

Each lab runs its test server on the device itself, so no network is in the way and the results show the app's own cost. A script ran the apps one at a time with the same Shadowsocks 2022 profile.

The score is 100 × (speed ÷ best speed)^0.3 × (best energy per GB ÷ energy per GB)^0.7. Efficiency weighs more because the top apps are already far faster than any internet connection.

Full method: Mac lab

MacBook Air M1, macOS 27.0.1 (susi connect, HyperOrbit and Tunna: macOS 26.7.1), on battery, screen off, no app windows open. Every app ran in TUN mode with the same Shadowsocks 2022 profile (2022-blake3-aes-128-gcm) to a sing-box server on the same Mac; iperf3 3.22 drove the traffic. A script ran the apps one at a time over two rounds, the second in reverse order. Speed: 5 s after a 2 s warm-up, over 1 and 4 connections. Efficiency: a steady 200 Mbit/s download for 20 s, with the energy, CPU time and memory of the app's tunnel process. Each test confirmed its traffic went through the tunnel; any test with keyboard or trackpad input was repeated. The sing-box command line is included as a baseline for the core alone. Versions as installed on 20–22 Oct 2026.

MTU is as macOS reported it (4000 requested where possible). Score = 100 × (speed ÷ best speed)^0.3 × (best energy per GB ÷ energy per GB)^0.7: efficiency weighs more because the top apps are already far faster than any internet connection.

Exceptions: Karing used its mixed stack. Happ ignores its MTU setting on the Mac. Hiddify 5.0.0 (a development build) ran as root with its fixed MTU of 9000. v2rayN chains two cores (sing-box, then Xray). Karing's 4-connection download and v2rayN's 1-connection upload are from one round. Tunna closes any connection idle for 10 s, which cut the test's own control connection, so we raised that limit to 300 s for its test.

Full method: iPhone lab

iPhone 11 Pro, iOS 27.0.1, on the USB cable at full charge, run overnight by an automated test with nobody touching it. A test app on the phone runs a Shadowsocks 2022 server (2022-blake3-aes-128-gcm) that every VPN app points at, so no Wi-Fi or internet is involved. Three rounds, apps in a different order each round. Speed: 5 s after a 2 s warm-up, over 1 and 4 connections. Efficiency: one download held at 200 Mbit/s for 120 s. Idle: 10 min with the screen locked. Versions as installed on 21 Oct 2026.

Power is the whole phone's, from its battery gauge; "extra" subtracts the same test without a VPN (0.68 W under load, 0.65 W idle). Energy per GB uses the speed actually held (Happ reached only 130 Mbit/s, V2Box 180). CPU and memory are the tunnel process's own. Idle power differences are mostly within noise (±0.07 W); wakeups show how often the tunnel woke the processor. Same score formula as the Mac lab.

Exception: one Hiddify round failed partway, so its speed and 200 Mbit/s figures are from two rounds.

sing-box (SFI) 1.14.7 was added on 22 Oct from one round, against no-VPN steps in the same session (0.83 W under load, 0.78 W idle). An Egern round in that session matched its earlier figures: speed within 2%, the same CPU, wakeups and memory, 0.06 W extra. Its profile sends apple.com traffic direct, so iOS could check the test app while the VPN was on.

Full method: real use

The app's own energy, as macOS estimates it for the app's processes, at a 200 Mbit/s download (J per GB × 25). Real path = through real servers over the internet; the 14–15 Oct runs were at 100 Mbit/s, assuming the same energy per GB. Lab = a test server on the same Mac.

Why MTU matters

At the common 1500, an app handles about 2.7× more packets per GB than at 4000. In our checks, raising the MTU from 1500 to 4000 lifted SFM's upload from 10.8 to about 14.3 Gbit/s (1.3×), and Karing's speed from 0.9 to 2.4 Gbit/s (2.5×, with its stack also changed) while its CPU time per GB fell by about a fifth. On the Mac, the seven apps at 1500 or below are all among the nine slowest. We set 4000 wherever an app allows it.

What we can't tell you

  • No phone battery measurement exists for any proxy protocol. The only phone battery figures cover five VPN protocols on one Android phone in 2022.
  • There is no independent Xray vs sing-box vs mihomo benchmark. The Shadowsocks 2022 loopback speed claim comes from tachyon-core's own author-run benchmark.
  • The field-test figures are single runs on one path (relay to a US exit, October 2026), not a controlled study.
  • Censorship reports are mostly user posts on net4people, often one ISP and a few people. net4people has had no new field reports on what works in Iran since early August 2026.
4 smaller limits
  • Brook, the Cloudflare quick tunnel, the HTTP header disguise and WARP exits come mainly from an Iranian how-to blog (ivpn.pro, 2024–2026) that also sells servers. Its claims are untested; the notes mark which ones independent sources support.
  • Egypt, UAE, Myanmar and Pakistan have almost no protocol-level data, so they're left out of the table.
  • App counts come from two app lists that lean toward Xray (one is Xray's own README).
  • The popular / cutting edge / legacy rules are this site's own; VMess, AnyTLS, XHTTP, AmneziaWG, Brook, HTTPUpgrade, MASQUE and Cloak are borderline calls.

Claims we checked

Common claims about iPhone VPN battery use and app internals, checked against code, Apple documentation and measurements. Open a claim to read the finding.

iOS

✓ HoldsBuilt-in IKEv2: IKE in userspace, ESP data path in the XNU kernel

The kernel ipsec interface and ESP crypto are confirmed in XNU. A rare kpipe mode can involve a user process. The userspace daemon names are not documented by Apple.

XNU if_ipsec.cXNU esp_core.c
~ PartlyNAT keepalives are offloaded to the baseband so the CPU sleeps and battery is saved

Apple offloads keepalives "to hardware" during sleep, only for supervised Always-On IKEv2, and calls it a battery cost. "Baseband" appears in no Apple source. Defaults are 20 s on Wi-Fi and 110 s on cellular.

apple/device-management VPN schemaApple Deployment: IKEv2
✓ HoldsiOS IKEv2 supports MOBIKE

On by default; disableMOBIKE since iOS 9. Apple publishes no savings figure.

apple/device-management VPN schema
✓ HoldsThird-party VPNs copy every packet between kernel and user space

Packets are passed as NSData arrays through NEPacketTunnelFlow. They come in batches, so "one context switch per packet" overstates it. Apple has published no benchmark.

NEPacketTunnelFlow
~ PartlyPacket tunnel extensions have a 50 MB memory limit (was 15 MB), enforced by jetsam

Apple DTS measured 50 MiB on iOS 15/16 and reports it unchanged on iOS 26, but it isn't officially documented. The 15 MB figure, the limit through iOS 14, comes from a 2017 developer observation. Jetsam enforcement is shown only by logs.

Apple Dev Forums 73148sing-box #3976
✕ WrongApple Silicon has a dedicated AES engine that IKEv2 uses

Kernel ESP calls corecrypto in software on the CPU. The AES Engine sits in the DMA path between flash storage and memory, for file encryption. Apple doesn't say IPsec uses it.

XNU corecrypto_aes.cApple Platform Security
~ PartlyChaCha20 runs on NEON; AES-GCM is faster on Apple chips

On an M2 Pro (ring), AES-256-GCM encrypted 2–3× faster per core. wolfSSL's tuned M1 build was 1.5× faster at encryption but about 20% slower at decryption, and its stock build was 3× slower. No cited source covers the NEON part. Throughput only; nobody has measured energy.

Vardanian 2025wolfSSL 2021
✓ HoldsiOS IKEv2 supports AES-GCM

AES-256-GCM since iOS 8.3, ChaCha20-Poly1305 since iOS 13. The default is AES-256-CBC. AES-128-GCM has been deprecated since iOS 17.

NEVPNProtocolIKEv2NEVPNIKEv2EncryptionAlgorithm
~ PartlyL2TP still supported; PPTP removed; iOS 26 broke legacy L2TP

L2TP is still a valid VPNType and PPTP is not. A VPN vendor's blog says iOS 26 dropped DES/3DES, SHA-1 and DH groups 1–13 from the built-in client, which breaks old gateways, L2TP ones included. Apple hasn't documented this.

apple/device-management VPN schemaVPN Tracker blog

Protocol

✓ HoldsL2TP/IPsec double encapsulation adds overhead

L2TP/UDP/IP adds 40 bytes, and only IPsec encrypts. It drew the highest battery current of the kernel-based protocols in the 5G poster.

Cisco L2TP MTUFed4FIRE 5G VPN battery poster, 2022
✓ HoldsWireGuard is silent when idle; keepalive is off by default, 25 s suggested

The silence ends once PersistentKeepalive is on. Whitepaper §5.1 "Silence is a Virtue" is about stealth, not idle behaviour.

WireGuard quickstartWireGuard whitepaper
✓ HoldsWireGuard uses ChaCha20-Poly1305 only, with no negotiation

It "intentionally lacks cipher and protocol agility".

WireGuard whitepaper
✓ HoldsHysteria 2 Brutal ignores loss; it falls back to BBR; Salamander XORs packets

Brutal does more than ignore loss: it sends more to hold its target rate. BBR is the default when no bandwidth is set.

~ PartlyUserspace QUIC costs more CPU than TCP+TLS, and iOS has no GSO

Up to 45.2% lower throughput than TCP+TLS on fast links. quic-go's GSO is Linux-only. Apple documents no UDP GSO either way.

Zhang et al., WWW '24 (QUIC vs TCP)quic-go optimizations
~ PartlyXTLS Vision avoids double encryption via splice

Splice is Linux-only and needs a pure-TCP inbound, so it never applies on an iOS client. The docs don't say "no double encryption".

Xray VLESS docs
✓ HoldsShadowsocks-2022 cipher set

AES-GCM methods are required and ChaCha is optional, for CPUs without AES instructions. No benchmarks.

Shadowsocks 2022 (SIP022)
? UnverifiedMITM decryption has a large CPU and battery cost

The mechanism is real: TLS is terminated and re-encrypted for each decrypted host. Nobody has measured the cost.

The sing-box docs make no performance or head-of-line-blocking statements.

Radio

~ PartlyThe radio tail lasts 10–15 s, set by the carrier

It is set by the carrier, but measured values run 5.0–11.6 s, and two NSA low-band networks show a second timer (12.1 s and 18.8 s). "10–15 s" leaves out T-Mobile 4G at 5.0 s.

Huang, MobiSys 2012Narayanan, SIGCOMM 2021
✕ WrongThe tail draws 1.5–2.5 W; idle draws under 0.05 W

Measured tail averages are 1.06 W (2011 LTE), 0.07–0.18 W (2021 4G), 0.25–0.6 W (2021 low-band 5G) and 1.09 W (mmWave). The idle part is right: 31 mW on LTE.

Huang, MobiSys 2012Narayanan, SIGCOMM 2021
✓ Holds5G NR has an RRC_INACTIVE state

Defined in 3GPP TS 38.331; the authors believe it appears on T-Mobile SA (their Fig. 10). NSA networks don't have it.

Narayanan, SIGCOMM 2021
✓ HoldsKeepalives keep the radio out of idle, and the tail dominates energy

Tail is 48% of LTE radio energy. One packet costs 12.76 J on LTE. Keepalive timers under 5 min cost over 17% of a day's battery (3G).

Huang, MobiSys 2012Wang, SIGCOMM 2011
~ PartlyIPsec NAT-T keepalive is 20 s; carrier NATs need 20–30 s

RFC 3948 sets 20 s by default. RFC 4787 asks for at least 2 minutes, but 74% of measured carrier-grade NATs expire idle UDP within 60 s (range 10–200 s; median 65 s cellular, 35 s fixed). No source gives 20–30 s.

RFC 3948RFC 4787Richter, IMC 2016
