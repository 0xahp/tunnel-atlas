Real use costs more. These labs measure one proxy with no rules. In an earlier test through real servers, two chained proxies with routing rules used about 3× the energy per GB of a single proxy (1.8–3.1× across the apps that kept the same version).

MTU changes results a lot. At the common 1500, an app handles about 2.7× more packets per GB than at 4000. In our checks, raising the MTU from 1500 to 4000 lifted SFM's upload from 10.8 to about 14.3 Gbit/s (1.3×), and Karing's speed from 0.9 to 2.4 Gbit/s (2.5×, with its stack also changed) while its CPU time per GB fell by about a fifth. On the Mac, the seven apps at 1500 or below are all among the nine slowest. We set 4000 wherever an app allows it.

How we tested: MacBook Air M1, macOS 27.0.1 (susi connect, HyperOrbit and Tunna: macOS 26.7.1), on battery, screen off, no app windows open. Every app ran in TUN mode with the same Shadowsocks 2022 profile (2022-blake3-aes-128-gcm) to a sing-box server on the same Mac; iperf3 3.22 drove the traffic. A script ran the apps one at a time over two rounds, the second in reverse order. Speed: 5 s after a 2 s warm-up, over 1 and 4 connections. Efficiency: a steady 200 Mbit/s download for 20 s, with the energy, CPU time and memory of the app's tunnel process. Each test confirmed its traffic went through the tunnel; any test with keyboard or trackpad input was repeated. The sing-box command line is included as a baseline for the core alone. Versions as installed on 20–22 Oct 2026.

MTU is as macOS reported it (4000 requested where possible). Score = 100 × (speed ÷ best speed)^0.3 × (best energy per GB ÷ energy per GB)^0.7: efficiency weighs more because the top apps are already far faster than any internet connection.

Exceptions: Karing used its mixed stack. Happ ignores its MTU setting on the Mac. Hiddify 5.0.0 (a development build) ran as root with its fixed MTU of 9000. v2rayN chains two cores (sing-box, then Xray). Karing's 4-connection download and v2rayN's 1-connection upload are from one round. Tunna closes any connection idle for 10 s, which cut the test's own control connection, so we raised that limit to 300 s for its test.

How we tested: iPhone 11 Pro, iOS 27.0.1, on the USB cable at full charge, run overnight by an automated test with nobody touching it. A test app on the phone runs a Shadowsocks 2022 server (2022-blake3-aes-128-gcm) that every VPN app points at, so no Wi-Fi or internet is involved. Three rounds, apps in a different order each round. Speed: 5 s after a 2 s warm-up, over 1 and 4 connections. Efficiency: one download held at 200 Mbit/s for 120 s. Idle: 10 min with the screen locked. Versions as installed on 21 Oct 2026.

Power is the whole phone's, from its battery gauge; "extra" subtracts the same test without a VPN (0.68 W under load, 0.65 W idle). Energy per GB uses the speed actually held (Happ reached only 130 Mbit/s, V2Box 180). CPU and memory are the tunnel process's own. Idle power differences are mostly within noise (±0.07 W); wakeups show how often the tunnel woke the processor. Same score formula as the Mac lab.

Exception: one Hiddify round failed partway, so its speed and 200 Mbit/s figures are from two rounds.

sing-box (SFI) 1.14.7 was added on 22 Oct from one round, against no-VPN steps in the same session (0.83 W under load, 0.78 W idle). An Egern round in that session matched its earlier figures: speed within 2%, the same CPU, wakeups and memory, 0.06 W extra. Its profile sends apple.com traffic direct, so iOS could check the test app while the VPN was on.

Independent, non-commercial testing on one Mac and one iPhone, October 2026. Results depend on the device, the OS and the app version; a real network adds its own costs.
